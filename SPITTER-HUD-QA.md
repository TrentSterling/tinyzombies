# Tiny Zombies: Spitter boundary qualification

The outline is the ground-plane boundary for the player's center, not the visible puddle's size. The pool radius is 71/64 metres; the existing player test extends it by 0.25 metres. This patch changes the pre-impact warning only. It does not change the 8,192-actor capacity, damage or grace, pool allocation, committed target, readback cadence, or Brute warning.

Use a foreground WebGPU browser on candidate runtime `302bbc7a75f07c542b50bdeec3305ff27ac0985a` and baseline `23ee4e77f1700a3ea1d8682e3d22312ccdcdfb60`. Use normal horde play first; a reproducible fixture may isolate one Spitter, but label that separately.

- With a live Spitter whose shot is committed, qualify default zoom 34 and close zoom 16 at 1280×720. Repeat at zoom 62 and a small viewport. Compare visible outline, title clearance, and canvas clipping. The direction of the radius change is not universal across viewport sizes.
- Stationary boundary and damage check: use the committed target itself, not the Spitter’s position or original aim point. On otherwise identical attempts, place the player center 1.2 m and 1.5 m from it along the screen-right ground direction, then repeat along the ground-depth axis. Where practical, position the player before the cue; confirm the GPU view and HUD settle before measuring. Do not accept a moving or shaking frame as a radius check. Verify the inner marker is inside and can take acid damage after landing, and the outer is outside and takes no acid damage. Use an available pool slot, no current hurt grace, and rule out other damage. Record actual HP and the Spitter state instead of inferring damage from pixels.
- Separate motion check: at close and default zoom, record matched frames with a stable committed target as the player crosses its screen-right and ground-depth directions. Compare the ring center with that target rendered using the world camera in the same frame, first without shake and then during combat shake. Record center offset separately from radius error, along with zoom, render width, player speed and shake condition. Do not substitute a second marker using the same HUD transform as the reference.
- Repeat muted, on a fresh restart, and with a target near the viewport edge. Verify no old-run warnings flash and new warnings still work muted.
- Do not treat the ring as evidence an acid pool landed. Its existing actor snapshot can lag; a committed shot can also land after the source dies even if its HUD cue disappears first. Those are separate behaviors, unchanged here.

The synthetic 60 Hz fixture (5 m/s, one readback per five frames delivered one frame later, no shake) gave 0.44–0.50 m of HUD camera lag after warmup, roughly 16–19 px at zoom 34 or 35–40 px at zoom 16 before HUD backing scaling at a 1280 px render width. That exceeds the stationary checks’ 0.14–0.16 m margin. It is not a measured in-game offset.

Source/Canvas-stub tests can prove geometry and guard parity. They do not replace a rendered warning and damage playtest.
